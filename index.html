Cloud underlighting at sunset

To-scale animation of Earth's shadow rising past three cloud bases at 6 km after sunset. A cloud base is lit from below while the sun is below its local horizon but the base is still above the shadow edge.

Animation

What you are looking at

  • Observer stands directly below the centre cloud. At t = 0 the sun is 2.27° below the observer's horizon, so the grazing ray (the edge of Earth's shadow) passes 5 km above the observer.
  • Clouds: three bases at 6 km, centred 29 km west, at, and 29 km east of the observer, each 10 km wide and aligned with its own local horizontal.
  • Grazing ray (bold amber, with arrow) is the sun ray that just touches the Earth at the sunset point; below it lies Earth's shadow (grey).
  • Sun rays (thin amber) are drawn at 1 km intervals, measured along the observer's vertical from the grazing ray.
  • Lit base (orange-red) marks the part of each cloud base that is lit from below.

Frames run from t = −11 min to t = +4.5 min in 0.25 min steps. Full derivations and assumptions are in the geometry notes.

Spherical Earth (R = 6371 km), no refraction, constant rate of solar descent. Source: github.com/cramshorn2/cloud-underlighting.
